# SubRotato handoff (named Sub Rotator until 2026-10-19) (as of 2026-10-01)

## Where we are
A finished static tool, v0.3, working and tested (325 tests). It was built in a claude.ai chat and published as a private artifact. This folder moves it into a real project so it can get live data and a public URL.

The app is now a dashboard (icon rail on desktop, bottom tabs on phones) that opens on one screen, This month: tap what you pay for, add what you want, and the answer appears beside it (menu: This month, Your plan; Steps: 1 Services, 2 My list; More: New releases) with live TVMaze title search and a Coming soon tab fed by public/upcoming.json. Next up: deploy to Vercel (owner OK needed), then the polish list.

## What exists in public/app.html (index.html until 2026-10-21)

**1. Rotation planner** (`PURE` block)
- Input: services with monthly price, a queue of titles with hours, an "available from" month per service (`earliest`), and hours per month the person can spend (`H`, 5 to 160, step 5).
- `simulate(svcs, order, thr, H)` runs month by month for 12 months. Each month it first spends hours on services already started, then starts new ones if there is room (`thr` is the minimum share of the month's hours a new service must fill, unless it can finish). Cost counts the price once for every service-month with any hours.
- `plan()` tries every priority order (Heap's algorithm) for up to 7 services. For more, it tries 4 sorted heuristics plus 2,500 seeded shuffles. Thresholds tried: 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75. Score = cost + unfinished hours x 1000 + a tiny finish-time term.
- Output panel: savings vs keeping everything all year, a "do this first" list (keep, subscribe, cancel), a 12-month timeline, month-by-month cards, cost per hour with Worth it / Fair / Pricey badges, and a copyable text plan.

**2. Statement import** (`CSV` block). Parses a bank or card CSV in the browser.
- `parseCSV` handles BOM, comma/semicolon/tab/pipe, quoted fields, CRLF.
- `detectColumns` finds a header row (preamble rows allowed) or infers columns from content when there is no header. Handles single amount columns, debit/credit columns, and either sign convention (majority sign is treated as spending).
- `parseAmount` and `parseDateStr` handle $1,234.56, (12.34), 12.34-, 19,99, 1.234,56, ISO, m/d/y, d/m/y (auto-detected), and "Sep 7, 2026".
- `FAMILIES` matches Netflix, Disney+, Hulu, HBO Max, Peacock, Apple TV, Paramount+, Crunchyroll, Game Pass, PS Plus. Tier is picked by nearest list price (0.6x to 1.7x band). A single annual PS Plus charge maps to the monthly list price. Loose matches (PSN, Sony Interactive) count only if they recur monthly.
- Other recurring charges: same merchant key, $2 to $80, at least two charges about 24 to 38 days apart with similar amounts, and not more than 1.6 charges per month. These show unchecked.
- "Try a sample" builds a fake statement. A paste box covers phones that cannot download CSVs.

**3. Time estimator** (`EST` block). Takes sleep, work, commute, school, kids and caregiving, and chores out of a 168-hour week, then counts 60% of the rest for shows and games. Presets cover work (none, part-time, full-time, long commute, two jobs or shifts), school, and home (kids under 6, kids 6 to 17, caring for a family member, home with kids all day). Seven fine-tune sliders. Default (full-time, no kids) gives 75 hours a month, about 2.5 hours a day. Checked 2026-10-11 against the BLS American Time Use Survey 2025 (released June 25, 2026; next release about June 2027): full-time workers average about 4.0 leisure hours a day and 2.4 hours of TV plus games and leisure computer use (Table 11A); employed adults with no children under 18 average 4.53 (Table 8B); sleep averages about 8.8 h including naps. The checked defaults: sleep 8.5 h, share 60%, two jobs or long shifts 50 h, full-time student 20 h, home with kids all day +10 h, chores 5 h a day (6 when not working or studying). The default lands at about 4.1 free hours a day.

**4. Catalog prices and tiers** (US, monthly, before tax, new-subscriber prices). All verified online on 2026-10-09/10 against each service's own pricing pages, then re-checked by an independent second pass; none is an estimate any more.
- Game Pass: Essential 9.99, Premium 14.99, Ultimate 22.99 (cut from 29.99 on 2026-04-21), PC Game Pass 13.99 (cut from 16.49). Day-one games are on Ultimate and PC Game Pass only; Premium gets Xbox-published games within about a year (not Call of Duty). Ultimate includes Premium, Essential and PC Game Pass.
- PS Plus: Essential 10.99, Extra 16.99, Premium 19.99 (all raised 2026-05-20; 12-month prices unchanged at 79.99 / 134.99 / 159.99). Premium includes Extra includes Essential. Sony first-party games are not on PS Plus at launch.
- Netflix with ads 8.99, Standard 19.99. Disney+ with ads 12.49, Premium 21.49; Hulu with ads 12.49, No Ads 21.49 (new prices; existing members from their first bill on or after 2026-10-21). HBO Max Basic with ads 10.99, Standard 18.49. Peacock Premium 12.99, Premium Plus 19.99 (Select 8.99 is left out: it has no movies, Originals or sports). Apple TV 14.99. Paramount+ Essential 8.99, Premium 13.99. Crunchyroll Fan 9.99, Mega Fan 13.99.
- In the catalog, each higher plan lists the lower plans it includes (`covers`). The planner counts only the highest plan in a month where both are active, and "Do this first" says Upgrade or Step down instead of subscribe-and-cancel.
- Watch for: HBO Max and Paramount+ may merge after the Paramount and WBD deal (reported closed around 2026-10-06), so those prices and tiers may change soon.

## Decisions already made
- No account, no bank link, no server round trip for statements. This is the main hook.
- Static single file, no framework, no build.
- Subscriptions rotate month to month. Annual plans are not modeled.
- Games radar is upcoming releases only, with platforms. No playtime data is needed for unreleased games, so hours are user-entered.
- No API keys for now (decided 2026-10-06). TV comes from TVMaze in the browser. Movies and games come from the hand-kept `public/upcoming.json`, checked weekly. RAWG (games) and TMDB (movies) stay optional later upgrades.

## Release data (`public/upcoming.json`)
A list of upcoming shows, movies, and games, with a weekly check. Items: `id` (slug), `title`, `kind` (`tv`, `movie`, `game`, `sport`; a sport also has `sport`, `ends`, `gamesPerWeek` and `gameHours`, see Roadmap item 5), `date` (`YYYY-MM-DD` or `YYYY-MM`), `confidence` (`confirmed`, `unconfirmed`, `rumored`), `checked`, and optional `service` (a family key such as `netflix`, `disney`, `gamepass`), `channel`, `platforms`, `subs`, `subsNote`, `hours`, `hoursSource`, `anime` (true for anime shows and movies), `episodes`, `release`, `firstDrop`, `perWeek`, `finale`, `season`, `tvmazeId`, `source`. `subs` lists the lowest tier of each subscription that includes the title at launch (catalog ids, for example `["gp-ult","gp-pc"]` for a day-one Game Pass game); `[]` means buy only; leave it out when nobody knows. The app suggests the cheapest listed tier, so a game that only needs a basic tier defaults to the basic tier. Unknown keys fail validation, so typos surface. `npm run refresh:dry` checks it (and looks for new titles) without writing; `npm run refresh` writes.

As of 2026-10-19 it holds 184 titles: 110 shows (40 anime, 13 K-dramas), 43 movies, 31 games (20 indie); 48 are tagged with where they were made. On 2026-10-11 it held 75. The 55 shows and movies added that day were found by four researchers (one per group of services) and each re-checked by an independent skeptic before going in; rumored dates are estimates from studio release windows and say so.

What the weekly script does (`scripts/refresh-upcoming.mjs`, zero dependencies):
- TV items with a `tvmazeId`: one TVMaze request each. Updates date, confidence, channel, service, and hours. Confidence: "confirmed" once TVMaze has a dated episode; "unconfirmed" while TVMaze says "In Development" or "To Be Determined", except that a date a person confirmed stays confirmed while TVMaze shows the same date; a season TVMaze has not dated keeps its label. Hours only when every episode length is known, and not while a season is still airing with fewer episodes listed than 4 or than the season before (TVMaze often lists only the first few; The Lowdown S2 had 5 of 8). Undated episodes count toward the season. The app's TVMaze search uses the same rules (`tvSummary`). A channel we do not cover never clears a service someone set (FX maps to Hulu). For a returning show, set `season`; without it the date is the series premiere.
- Movies and games cannot be machine-checked. It flags them when the date has passed, is within 30 days and not checked for 7, or is not checked for 21 days.
- Drops anything that came out more than 30 days ago. A quiet run writes nothing; it only refreshes the `checked` stamp when it is 14 or more days old.
- With `--discover` (the npm scripts and the workflow pass it) it also looks for new titles, about 8 extra requests:
  - **TV, added automatically:** one call to TVMaze `/schedule/full` (every future episode, ~15 MB, ~3 s) finds season premieres in the next 90 days on the services we cover, English, TVMaze popularity 95+, no talk, news, game or award shows, at most 6 a week, most popular first. They land with the plan set to the cheapest one, so check the plan in the pull request (a Showtime show on Paramount+ needs Premium).
  - **Movies, suggested only:** Wikipedia's "List of <service> original films" pages (MediaWiki API, keyless) give dated films for the next 180 days that the list lacks, English only, no stand-up, specials or documentaries. They go in the report as a checklist with ready-to-paste JSON, never into the file. Netflix's page is thorough; the others are thin, so theatrical films heading to Disney+, HBO Max, Peacock and Paramount+ still need a person.
  - Set `REFRESH_CONTACT` (the workflow sets the repo URL) so the User-Agent carries a contact, as Wikimedia asks.
- Hard limits: 90 requests (raised from 60 on 2026-10-16, when anime took the list to about 55 shows), 600 ms between requests, one retry on 429 or 5xx (waits the server's Retry-After, at most 20 s), stops after 3 failures in a row, 4 minute ceiling. Exits non-zero only if it could not check anything it should have.

The GitHub workflow (`.github/workflows/refresh-upcoming.yml`) runs it Mondays 14:17 UTC:
- Cost: one job, no installs, shallow checkout, 5 minute hard stop, no overlapping runs. About 1 billed minute a run, so 4 to 5 a month. Public repos do not use the allowance at all. Pause it for free with a repo variable `UPCOMING_REFRESH` = `off` (a skipped job is not billed).
- Real changes (including shows it added) open or update one pull request (`bot/upcoming`). A change that is only the `checked` date is committed straight to the default branch. Items needing a human, suggested movies, and anything the script could not reach go in one issue labeled `upcoming-recheck`, updated each week and closed when clear.
- Saying no: put a title (or `tvmaze:<id>`) in the optional top-level `skip` list in `public/upcoming.json`. Discovery never adds or suggests it again, so a deleted auto-added show does not come back next week and a declined film stops keeping the issue open.
- Discovery follows the same limits as the TV checks: the request cap, stop after 3 failures in a row, and each request's timeout (plus its one retry) fits in the time left. Wikipedia error answers (missing page, rate limited) are named in the report.
- One-time setup (owner): put the project on GitHub, then Settings > Actions > General > Workflow permissions > allow Actions to create pull requests. If the default branch is protected, allow the bot to push or accept that the `checked`-only commits will fail.
- Free minutes are per account and shared across private repos (2,000 a month on GitHub Free as last known; confirm under Settings > Billing).

- **Movies made for a service:** the service's press site (Netflix Tudum, Disney+ / D23, Apple TV press, HBO Max / WBD press, Peacock blog, Paramount+ press), then Wikipedia's per-service film lists (automatic suggestions), What's on Netflix, What's on Disney Plus.

## Data source facts (re-verify current terms before relying on them)
- **TVMaze**: free, no key, 20 calls per 10 seconds per IP, CORS enabled, CC BY-SA 4.0 so credit is required. Shows carry `webChannel` for streaming originals.
- **TMDB**: free key, non-commercial use only with attribution. A commercial plan is reported at about $149 a month for small companies. Watch-provider data is JustWatch's and needs its credit. Terms limit how long data can be cached (reported as six months).
- **RAWG**: free key, 20,000 requests a month, non-commercial, backlink required. Gives release dates, platforms, and average playtime.
- **IGDB**: fallback. Twitch developer account and OAuth, server-side only, about 4 requests a second.
- **Game Pass and PS Plus catalogs**: no official API found, only community scrapers.

## Known issues and limits
- Rotation assumes you can cancel and rejoin monthly at list price. No promos or tax. Bundles and yearly prices are tips only; the plan still prices each month at list price.
- Planner is a greedy simulation with a permutation search. Not guaranteed optimal above 7 services.
- Statement import is US-centric (prices, defaults). "Other monthly charges" can include a store visited about once a month. Apple.com/bill is ambiguous and is not matched to Apple TV.
- Release dates differ between calendars. Example: one calendar lists Rayman Legends Retold on Oct 1 and another on Dec 3; Ace Combat 8 shows as Oct 2 in some and Oct 8 in others. Hence the "last checked" stamp.
